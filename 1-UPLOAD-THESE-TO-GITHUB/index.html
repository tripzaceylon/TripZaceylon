      Festivals

      The EsalaPerahera

      For ten nights every Esala moon, Kandy gives its streets over to a procession that has run for more than a thousand years — fire dancers, whip-crackers, Kandyan drummers, and tuskers in embroidered caparisons carrying a replica of the sacred tooth relic.

      It usually falls in late July or August. If your dates are anywhere near it, tell us early: the whole city books out, and the good balcony seats go months ahead.

      Plan around the Perahera

      Festivals

      Vesakthe festival of light

      On the May full moon the island marks the Buddha’s birth, enlightenment and passing all at once. Every doorway hangs a hand-made lantern, and whole streets are given over to pandals — vast illuminated panels telling stories in light, wired up by neighbourhoods that compete quietly all year.

      The other half of Vesak is dansal: strangers setting up roadside stalls to hand out food and drink for nothing, all night, to anyone walking past. Colombo and Kandy are the spectacle; a small town is the better memory.

      Plan around Vesak

      Festivals

      AvuruduSinhala & Tamil New Year

      Sri Lanka’s new year falls on 13–14 April and runs on astrological timing rather than the clock — the minute to light the hearth, the minute to eat, even the direction to face while doing it, all published in advance and followed to the letter.

      Between the rituals it is kavum, kokis and kiribath, oil-lamp games in the garden, and the entire country effectively closed for a week. Travelling then means quiet roads and shut kitchens, so we plan around it carefully.

      Most nationalities need an ETA (Electronic Travel Authorisation) before arrival, applied for online and usually approved within a day or two. Rules and any fee waivers change from time to time, so confirm on the official immigration portal close to your travel date — we send every guest the current link and check the application with you.

      There is no bad season — only a different coast. December to April is best for the west and south coasts and the cultural triangle; May to September suits the east coast at Arugam Bay, Trincomalee and Pasikuda. The hill country is pleasant year-round and coolest from December to February.

      Ten to fourteen days is the sweet spot — enough for the cultural triangle, the hill country by train, one safari and four or five days by the sea without spending your holiday in a vehicle. Seven days works if you pick two regions instead of four.

      Yes — it's one of Asia's more relaxed destinations for both, with very low rates of serious crime against visitors and a strong culture of hospitality towards children. Normal precautions apply: watch your belongings in crowded transport hubs, take licensed transport at night, and swim only where a lifeguard or your guide advises, as some beaches have strong rip currents.

      The Sri Lankan Rupee (LKR). Hotels, larger restaurants and city shops take Visa and Mastercard, but you'll want cash for tuk-tuks, village markets, entrance tips and small cafés. ATMs are widely available in towns; withdraw before heading into national parks or remote hill villages.

      Shoulders and knees covered, shoes and hats removed before entering, and no posing with your back to a Buddha statue. Carry a light scarf — it doubles as sun cover. Tattoos of the Buddha are best kept covered, as they can cause genuine offence.

      Most of our guests use a private driver-guide for the flexibility, with one or two legs done by train because the Kandy–Ella line is worth the trip in itself. Self-drive is possible but rarely enjoyable: roads are busy, unwritten rules dominate, and a local driver costs less than you'd expect.

      It can be genuinely hot, but every kitchen will dial it down if you ask for "less chilli". Vegetarians and vegans eat exceptionally well here — a standard rice and curry is mostly plant-based, built on dhal, jackfruit, beans, gotukola and coconut. Tell us about allergies and we brief every stop in advance.

      Yes, and we only work with operators who keep their distance, cap vehicle numbers and never bait. Leopards are best in Yala and Wilpattu, elephant gatherings peak at Minneriya between roughly July and October, and blue and sperm whales pass Mirissa from December to April and Trincomalee from May to September. We don't offer elephant rides or captive-animal attractions.

      Local SIM and eSIM data is cheap and fast in towns and along the coast, with patchier coverage deep in the hills and inside national parks. Hotels almost all have Wi-Fi. We help you set up a SIM at the airport on arrival if you want one.
