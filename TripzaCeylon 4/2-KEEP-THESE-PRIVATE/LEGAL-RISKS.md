# Legal and compliance review — TripzaCeylon

**Date:** 12 September 2026
**Reviewer:** Claude (an AI assistant). **Not a lawyer. This is not legal advice.**

You asked me to "make no mistakes". I can't promise that, and you should be
suspicious of anyone who does. What I can tell you is exactly what I changed,
exactly what I couldn't verify, and which items genuinely need a Sri Lankan
lawyer before you take money from anyone.

Read section 1 first. It is the part that could have caused you real trouble.

## 1. The serious one — false credentials (FIXED, but read this)

Your site was publicly claiming six credentials. I removed all of them.

| Claim that was on the site | Why it mattered |
|---|---|
| "SLTDA Licensed Operator" | **Specific criminal offence if untrue** |
| "SLAITO Member" | Membership body — verifiable, and they defend it |
| "IATA Accredited" | IATA accreditation is a formal process with a numeric code |
| "Travelife Engaged" | Certification scheme with a register |
| "Secure Card Payments" | You cannot take card payments at all yet |
| "24/7 On-Trip Support" | A service promise you'd be contractually held to |

Under the **Tourism Act No. 38 of 2005**, tourist services in Sri Lanka must be
registered with the SLTDA (s.48(4)), and **s.48(10) makes it an offence for an
unregistered business to publish itself as registered.** Enforcement against
unregistered operators is active. A footer badge is publishing.

There was also a section headed *"Accreditations we hold"* above a wall of eight
organisation names — SLTDA, SLAITO, IATA, Travelife, Sri Lanka Railways,
Department of Wildlife Conservation, Ceylon Chamber of Commerce, Sri Lanka
Tourism Alliance. That reads as claiming membership of all eight. Removed, and
the heading rewritten to describe what you actually do.

> **You must register with the SLTDA before trading as a tour operator.** That is
> not a website issue, it is a licence-to-operate issue, and it comes before
> everything else in this document. Once registered, put the badge back with your
> registration number next to it — a number is worth more than a badge anyway.

## 2. Fabricated content (FIXED)

Removed, because publishing invented customer feedback is a deceptive trade
practice under the **Consumer Affairs Authority Act No. 9 of 2003**, and would be
an offence in several of the markets your visitors come from:

- **Five invented testimonials** attributed to named people — "Priya & Daniel R.",
  "Marcus Feld", "Aiko Tanaka", "The Okonkwo family", "Sofia & Marc" — with the
  standfirst *"Unedited lines from post-trip emails."* They were not.
- **"4.9 ★★★★★ from 412 reviews."** No reviews existed.
- **Partner counts:** 42 guesthouses, 28 driver-guides, 11 park ecologists,
  6 tea estates. All invented by me when I built the page.
- **Placeholder address** "42 Galle Face Terrace, Colombo 03" — now reads
  "Colombo, Sri Lanka — full address on request" until you supply the real one.

The whole Stories section is gone, along with its nav link, footer link and search
entry. **When you have genuine reviews, put them back** — with real names used
with permission, and a real count.

## 3. Claims I left, and why

These survived because they are defensible, but check I've got them right:

- **"Around 6,000 wild elephants"** — widely published figure, fine.
- **"One of the highest leopard densities on earth"** (Yala) — commonly stated in
  published sources, and hedged.
- **"Blue whales December–April"** — seasonal, stated as seasonal.
- **Driving times on the planner** — labelled as estimates on the page, and the
  Terms repeat it.
- **"We do not book elephant rides or bathing"** — a promise about your own
  conduct. Keep it true.

Watch out for: anything in the package descriptions promising a specific animal
sighting, a specific room, or a fixed price. Wildlife is wildlife.

## 5. Data protection

**Sri Lanka's Personal Data Protection Act No. 9 of 2022** is in force in stages.
Part V commenced July 2023; Parts VI and VIII–X in December 2023; the remaining
parts await a commencement date. The direction is settled even if the timing
isn't, so the privacy policy is written to the full standard rather than the
current minimum.

**The GDPR may also apply to you.** Your site is in English, sells to European
travellers, and lists European visitors as your market. That can bring you inside
Article 3(2). A Sri Lankan lawyer with data protection experience should confirm.

### What I changed

- **Consent checkbox added** to the enquiry form, unticked by default, and the
  form refuses to send without it. A pre-ticked box is not consent.
- **Marketing opt-in is a separate, optional box** — bundling "contact me about
  this enquiry" with "send me offers" is the single most common consent mistake.
- **Consent is recorded**, not assumed: `consent_at` timestamp and a `marketing`
  boolean go into the database with each enquiry. The burden of proving consent
  is on you, so you need the evidence.
- **The notes field now warns** against sending passport, card, health or medical
  details. Those are special-category data and you don't want them arriving by
  email.

### Data minimisation

The form collects name, email, trip preferences and free text. That is
proportionate for a travel enquiry. **Do not add** date of birth, passport number,
nationality or address to the enquiry form — collect those at booking, over a
channel that suits them.

## 6. Cookies and tracking

**Good news: nothing to consent to.** I checked.

- No Google Analytics, no Meta pixel, no Hotjar, no tag manager. Nothing.
- No cookies set by the site at all.
- Browser storage is used only by the staff admin page, for a sign-in token that
  clears when the tab closes. Strictly necessary, and not for visitors.

**A cookie consent banner is therefore not required**, and adding one would be
theatre. One genuine caveat: **Google Fonts and Unsplash images are loaded from
their servers**, which transmits each visitor's IP address to Google and Unsplash.
German courts have found Google Fonts loading to need its own justification under
the GDPR.

**Recommended fix, and it's a small one:** download the two font files and the
photographs, and serve them from your own repository. That removes the transfer
entirely, makes the site faster, and removes the risk of a photo disappearing.
Flagged in `cookies.html` as a decision for you. I'd do it before any serious
launch.

## 7. Images and copyright

All 61 photographs come from Unsplash. The **Unsplash License** grants free
commercial use without attribution, so you are covered. Two caveats worth knowing:

- You cannot compile them into a competing photo service. You aren't.
- **Photos can be removed by their photographer.** You're hotlinking to Unsplash's
  CDN, so if one disappears you get a blank space. Another argument for hosting
  them yourself.

**The logo is yours** — you supplied the artwork, I only removed its background.

**One thing to verify:** several captions describe the *place* rather than the
activity, because Unsplash has no Sri Lankan photos of ziplining, paintball or
ATV riding. Those captions are honest, but check you're comfortable with, say, a
photograph of the Ella valley illustrating the zipline.

## 8. Accessibility

Legally this matters most for your European and North American customers.

### Fixed

- **Colour contrast.** Three palette values failed WCAG AA for normal text. The
  muted grey was 4.46:1 and the green label colour 4.11:1 (4.5:1 is the bar).
  White on the green buttons was 4.19:1. I darkened `--muted` and `--leaf`
  slightly; all now pass, and the design is visually unchanged.
- **Skip-to-content link** on every page, and a `<main>` landmark to skip to.
- **Visible focus ring** — keyboard users could previously tab through the site
  without seeing where they were.
- **Labelled the unlabelled controls** in the admin panel.

### Already good

- Every one of 64 images has an `alt` attribute; the three empty ones are
  decorative, which is correct.
- No `<div onclick>` — every interactive element is a real button or link, so
  keyboard and screen-reader support comes free.
- Every form field has a `<label for>`.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` is respected.
- `lang="en"` on every page.

### Still worth doing

- **Test with a keyboard.** Tab through the whole site. The map pins and the
  dragging rails are the places most likely to trap a keyboard user.
- **Test with a screen reader** — VoiceOver is built into your Mac (⌘ + F5).
- The interactive map is inherently visual. A text list of places sits beside it,
  which helps, but a blind user cannot use the map itself.

## 10. Other risks

**Social account security.** You pasted your TikTok and Instagram passwords into
a chat earlier in this project. Treat both as compromised: change them and turn on
two-factor authentication. Chat logs are stored, and that one is on me for not
insisting harder at the time.

**Your repository is public.** Anyone can read every file, including these notes.
Nothing in them is secret — but never commit a password.

**No payment handling exists.** Good, for now. The moment you take card payments
you're into PCI-DSS territory, which is why using a payment provider's hosted
page rather than building your own is the right answer.

**The admin page can be found.** It's at a guessable address on a public site. The
login is real — enforced by the database, not the page — so this is acceptable.
But use a strong, unique password.

## What I could not check

Said plainly, because the gaps matter as much as the findings:

- Whether you hold **any** of the removed credentials. If you do hold some, put
  those back with their registration numbers.
- Whether the company is incorporated, and under what name.
- Whether any Sri Lankan rules specific to travel agents — bonding, client money
  handling, trust accounts — apply to you. **Ask about client money specifically.**
- Current SLTDA registration fees, categories and lead times.
- Whether your insurance covers professional liability for travel arrangement.
- The precise commencement status of the remaining PDPA sections as of today.
